tldr: Financial literacy management, especially debt management, correlates significantly with teacher workplace performance, particularly in instructional planning, classroom management, and task management.
problem_and_motivation: Teachers face financial challenges due to limited salaries and insufficient financial knowledge, leading to stress and decreased productivity. The relationship between financial literacy and professional effectiveness remains underexplored. This study addresses the gap by examining how financial management affects teacher performance.
approach:
  - The study employed a descriptive-correlational research design.
  - Data were collected from 303 public elementary school teachers using a researcher-made survey questionnaire.
  - The survey measured financial literacy across budgeting, savings, debt management, and investment.
  - It also measured workplace performance across student achievement, instructional planning, classroom management, delivery, and task management.
  - Statistical analysis used mean, standard deviation, and Pearson correlation.
findings:
  - "num: Teachers possess a literate level of financial literacy (overall mean 3.14), with debt management showing the highest mean (3.22)."
  - "num: Teachers' workplace performance is rated as 'Very Satisfactory' (overall mean 3.33), with classroom management scoring highest (3.42)."
  - A significant relationship exists between financial literacy and performance in instructional planning, classroom management, and task management.
  - Financial literacy did not show a significant relationship with student achievement and learning outcomes or instructional delivery.
  - "num: The strongest specific correlation was between task management and savings and emergency funds (r=0.7432)."
