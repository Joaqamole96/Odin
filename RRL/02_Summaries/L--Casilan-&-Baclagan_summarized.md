tldr: Lower middle-income Filipino parents view children helping out as a non-obligatory practice that should originate from the child's autonomous decision and willingness.
problem_and_motivation: The study addresses the lack of parental perspectives on children's "helping out" in Filipino lower middle-income families. Understanding these expectations is crucial as cultural norms around filial obligation may be shifting. The research aims to document how parents identify children capable of helping and the reasons behind this practice.
approach:
  - This qualitative exploratory study used semi-structured interviews with 10 parents from lower middle-income families.
  - Participants had two or more children, lived in NCR, Region III, or Region IV-A, and had monthly incomes between ₱21,194 and ₱43,828.
  - Data were analyzed using thematic analysis with open, axial, and selective coding to derive core themes and subthemes.
  - The study ensured ethical compliance including informed consent per the Data Privacy Act of 2012 and used Turnitin for plagiarism checking.
  - The interview questions explored parental views on helping out, identification of supportive children, reasons for helping, and areas of expected support.
findings:
  - Parents consider helping out a non-obligatory practice and not a child's responsibility, but a parent's obligation to provide for the family.
  - The practice is valued culturally and generationally, but parents cautioned against it being made "toxic" through pressure or manipulation.
  - Key reasons for children to help include poverty, witnessing family struggles, utang na loob (debt of gratitude), and parents' aging.
  - Qualities of a capable child include being studious, financially capable, determined, and exhibiting kusang loob (willingness) and kabutihang loob (kindness).
  - Parents do not prioritize birth order, though the eldest is expected to be first to help; all children are seen as potential helpers if raised well.
