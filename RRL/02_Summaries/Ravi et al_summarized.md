tldr: "TRuST-M evaluates how explanation methods impact user trust and perceived effectiveness in LLM-based threat moderation for web and social-network environments."
problem_and_motivation: "LLM-based content moderation systems achieve high accuracy but lack interpretability, reducing user trust and accountability. Existing XAI methods are rarely evaluated for usability or impact in real-world moderation workflows. This gap hinders the development of transparent and trustworthy moderation tools for high-stakes online environments."
approach:
  - "A RoBERTa model was pretrained on 1M Telegram posts and fine-tuned on 15,063 labeled messages for three-class threat detection (No Threat, Judicial Threat, Non-Judicial Threat)."
  - "The model achieved 95.8% accuracy, weighted F1 of 0.96, and Cohen's kappa of 0.94 on a held-out test set."
  - "A within-subjects user study (n=31) was conducted using the Total Freedom Interface (TFI) to evaluate three explanation methods: Integrated Gradients, LIME, and attention visualization."
  - "Participants classified six messages of varying complexity and rated trust, explainability, usability, and moderation effectiveness for each explanation method."
  - "Spearman correlations and chi-squared tests were used to analyze relationships between explanation clarity, trust, usability, and demographic factors."
findings:
  - "LIME was preferred by 58% of participants (18/31) for its intuitive word-level highlights, though longer response times were noted."
  - "Attention visualizations were rated least helpful due to unclear token emphasis and misleading salience on non-informative tokens."
  - "num: A positive, statistically significant correlation was found between explanation clarity and trust (ρ = 0.493, p = 0.005)."
  - "num: Trust was strongly correlated with perceived moderation effectiveness (ρ = 0.62, p < 0.01)."
  - "num: Usability showed increasing correlations with Trust (ρ = 0.38, p < 0.05), Explainability (ρ = 0.57, p < 0.01), and Moderation Confidence (ρ = 0.72, p < 0.01)."
  - "Education level was associated with usability satisfaction; 83% of PhD respondents rated the system 'Excellent' or 'Best Imaginable' compared to 50% of Bachelor's degree holders."
  - "No significant effects on trust or explainability were found for gender, age, or prior AI/ML tool experience."
  - "Average interaction time varied by message difficulty, with an unexpected increase for 'easy' judicial threats, suggesting a confirmation-verification effect."
  - "Participants expressed interest in hybrid explanation formats combining the strengths of different methods."
  - "Qualitative feedback highlighted that response time for LIME is a barrier for real-world use."
