tldr: Urban poor families in Zamboanga City survive through informal labor, resource-sharing,
  and kinship networks, but government aid like 4Ps remains insufficient and irregular.
problem_and_motivation: The poorest families in Zamboanga City's slums face severe economic
  challenges due to limited access to formal employment, education, and healthcare.
  There is limited research on the everyday survival mechanisms of slum households in
  secondary Philippine cities. This study fills that gap by exploring the lived experiences
  of these families.
approach:
  - A qualitative phenomenological design was used to explore the lived experiences of
    the poorest families.
  - Data was collected via in-depth semi-structured interviews and focus group discussions
    with 30 households in three slum barangays.
  - Participatory observation provided additional insights into daily life and coping
    mechanisms.
  - Thematic analysis was applied to identify patterns related to survival strategies,
    social capital, and government support.
  - Social Capital Theory and New Public Management theory framed the analysis of survival
    strategies and government interventions.
findings:
  - Families rely on a combination of informal labor, resource-sharing, and kinship networks
    for economic survival.
  - num: 30 households were interviewed; most engaged in multiple informal activities
    like street vending and waste picking.
  - Social capital, particularly family and community ties, provides critical support
    through job referrals, mutual aid, and resource pooling.
  - Government programs like the 4Ps offer short-term relief but are insufficient due
    to irregular disbursements and inadequate funding.
  - Women and elderly members bear disproportionate economic and caregiving burdens,
    often engaging in low-barrier informal work.
  - The irregularity of informal income and limited government support perpetuate economic
    instability and vulnerability.
