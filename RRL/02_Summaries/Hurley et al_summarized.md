tldr: Financial literacy deficits among police officers stem from absent formal training and irregular income, impacting budgeting, saving, and job performance.
problem_and_motivation: The Philippine National Police (PNP) lacks formal financial education in its training curricula, leaving officers unprepared for budgeting, saving, and investing despite irregular income and high-stress conditions. Financial stress negatively impacts job performance, morale, and community trust, yet no systematic financial literacy program exists for this population.
approach:
  - The study used a mixed-methods design combining quantitative surveys and qualitative interviews with 50 PNP personnel in Dagupan City.
  - Data were collected using a four-part questionnaire covering demographics, economic factors, financial knowledge, attitudes, and behaviors.
  - Simple random sampling was used to select participants from the Dagupan City Police Station.
  - Descriptive statistics (mean, frequency, percentage) and a Likert scale were employed to assess financial literacy levels.
findings:
  - num: 42% of respondents were aged 31-40, and 92% were married, indicating a demographic with significant financial responsibilities.
  - num: 42% of participants were Police Staff Sergeants (PSSG), the most engaged group, suggesting mid-level officers seek financial education.
  - Officers demonstrated adequate financial knowledge in budgeting and saving but struggled with investing and long-term financial planning.
  - num: 60% of respondents had salary loans, indicating reliance on debt to meet financial needs.
  - num: 40% of participants use auto-debit for savings, while 32% review their financial goals monthly.
