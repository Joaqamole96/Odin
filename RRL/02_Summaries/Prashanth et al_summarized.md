tldr: "Adaptive buffering with uncertainty-based drift detection improves incremental learning for lifestyle disease prediction, with BiLSTM showing best retention and minimal forgetting under concept drift."
problem_and_motivation: "Static machine learning models degrade in performance under evolving health data distributions due to concept drift, yet systematic comparison of buffering strategies for incremental learning is lacking. Addressing this gap is critical for maintaining real-time prediction accuracy in clinical and public health applications. The study evaluates adaptive, FIFO, and no-buffer strategies to guide model updates under drift."
approach:
  - "Uses NHANES data (9,236 records, 26 features) with synthetic abrupt, gradual, and recurring drifts introduced via label flipping."
  - "Compares three buffering strategies: No Buffer (update on latest batch), FIFO (fixed-size sliding window), and Adaptive Buffer (retains only drifted batches)."
  - "Evaluates four deep learning architectures: BiLSTM, BNN, GRU, and LSTM for binary diabetes prediction."
  - "Implements three drift detection methods: statistical tests (KS, T-test), hybrid (accuracy dip + statistical), and uncertainty-based (Monte Carlo dropout)."
  - "Measures performance via accuracy, precision, recall, F1, AUC, runtime, retention, and forgetting scores."
findings:
  - "num: Adaptive buffer achieves 0.80 accuracy and 0.85 AUC for recurring drift, outperforming FIFO and No-Buffer."
  - "Hybrid drift detection with BiLSTM yields highest retention (ARA 0.7318) and lowest forgetting (0.0350) under FIFO buffer."
  - "Increasing drift magnitude from 20% to 100% reduces accuracy by up to 15% across models, but adaptive buffer mitigates decline."
  - "Uncertainty-based detection is resilient to high drift but incurs high computational cost, especially for BNN (up to 363 seconds)."
  - "GRU and LSTM offer faster runtime but lower drift detection counts compared to BiLSTM."
