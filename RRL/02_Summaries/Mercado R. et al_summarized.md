tldr: Filipino young professionals highly value occupational growth, intellectual achievement, material benefits, and organizational culture, with profile variables failing to predict these values.
problem_and_motivation: Understanding the work values of Filipino young professionals is crucial for organizational alignment and productivity. However, the relationship between demographic profile variables and these culturally specific values remains unclear.
approach:
  - The study employed a descriptive correlational survey with 100 randomly selected young professionals from Tarlac Province.
  - The Filipino Work Values Scale (FWVS), containing 80 items across 10 dimensions, was used to measure values on a 5-point Likert scale.
  - Profile variables such as sex, educational attainment, position, and salary were collected and described using frequency counts.
  - Spearman's Rho correlation coefficient was used to examine the relationship between profile variables and work values.
  - Multivariate regression analysis was conducted to determine if profile variables could predict Filipino work values.
findings:
  - The majority of respondents were female (59%), held bachelor's degrees (88.6%), and occupied rank-and-file positions (69.5%).
  - Work values rated with very high importance were occupational (mean 4.66), intellectual achievement (4.60), material (4.57), and organizational (4.52).
  - work values rated with high importance included environmental (4.43), managerial (4.39), religious (4.34), familial (4.32), interpersonal (4.27), and varied (4.23).
  - Spearman's Rho revealed weak but significant negative correlations between salary and organizational (rho=-0.2, p=.043), varied (rho=-0.209, p=.034), and interpersonal work values (rho=-0.204, p=.039).
  - Multivariate regression showed insufficient evidence that profile variables predict Filipino work values (p=.442, R²=5.8%).
