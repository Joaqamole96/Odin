Abstract

The impulse buying behavior of Generation Z (Gen Z) consumers has become imperative

for researchers and businesses aiming to thrive in today's market.   The egregious or shocking

intuitive observation among Gen Z consumers of becoming impulsive buyers concerns the

researchers.   The internal and external driving forces used in the study are psychological traits,

behavioral patterns, mental and emotional responses, platform, influencer impact, and purchasing

power as determinants of impulsive buying drawn from Hawkins-Stern's Impulse Buying

Theory.   The novelty and implication will shed light from local to international perspectives to

help stakeholders and companies in online business craft excellent marketing strategies and spot

opportunities to attract Gen. Z consumers.   A descriptive quantitative design evaluated the

relationship between the variables affecting Gen Z's impulse buying behavior.

The study revealed that when emotional response and behavioral patterns, purchasing

power followed by platforms is intensely used, it dramatically influences Gen Z's impulsive

buyers, as justified by Hawkins-Stern's Impulse Buying Theory caused Gen Z's unplanned

purchases to surpass their everyday purchases, and most likely become impulsive buyers.

Driven by instinct, mood, budget, emotional response, affective state supported by past buying

behavior, external stimuli, and online offerings lead to Gen Z's experiences impulsive buying to

reward themselves, yet with no intent of purchasing, which leads to unplanned purchases, thus

Introduction

Understanding the complexities of impulse buying among Gen Z has become one of the

observable top strategic priorities for firms looking to thrive in the modern market.   It delves

into the main reasons and influential factors behind Gen Z's impulse buying behavior, shedding

light on what drives their spontaneous purchasing decisions.   The shocking intuitive observation

that Gen Z consumers become impulsive buyers is of concern to researchers.   Hence, the

implication of this study from a global to local perspective can lead to greater business

opportunities and marketing strategies.   Many scholars and industry professionals have recently

focused on understanding impulse buying, alternatively termed "impulse shopping" (Kumar et

al., 2020).   Impulse buying, as defined by scholars (Iyer et al., 2020; Bandyopadhyay et al.,

2021; Cavazos-Arroyo & Máynez-Guaderrama, 2022; Nigam et al., 2022), refers to buying

things on the spur of the moment, without planning beforehand.   It involves quick decision-

making and little thought about the product or its consequences, and it is not just about fulfilling

immediate needs but also about expressing one's identity through the things one buys.   It is

about fitting into what is trending in the market, like the artisans or organic-green marketing

phenomenon (Belandres & Fontanilla, 2020).

Hawkins-Stern’s Impulsive Buying Theory

The theoretical underpinning of the study is grounded by the theory of Hawkins-Stern's

Impulsive Buying Theory proposed in 1962 by Hawkins.   He believed in impulse behavior and

argued that consumers indulge in impulsive buying behavior under the influence of external

forces and examples such as promotions, advertising, and word of mouth.   Stern created four

categories of impulse buying as stated by Sajeetha-Madhavan & Tay, L. (2023), namely: Pure

Impulse Buying is experienced when there is an escape purchase that breaks the regular buying

pattern and occurs when a customer sees a product or remembers an advertisement.   Reminder:

Impulse Buying is when the consumer has no intention of buying it but remembers that they need

it, which eventually leads to unplanned purchases.   A study by Barcelona et al. (2022) states that

Pure Impulse and Suggested Impulse Buying habits are experienced only with little impact by

TikTok's Shopee finds.   This is further explained by the respondents' planned impulse buying

and reminder impulse buying habits.   The next category is Suggested Impulse Buying, which

occurs when a consumer first sees a product and feels a need for it, then buys it.   Finally,

Planned Impulse Buying happens when the consumer makes a specific purchase decision based

on special prices, coupon offerings, visuals, promotional offers, and lower prices that lead to

impulse buying and are driven by their mood swing (mental and emotional state).   The study

conducted by Sajeetha-Madhavan and Tay (2023) stated that online impulse-buying behavior has

a favorable relationship with price, promotion, motivation, service quality, variety, and

convenience.   Similarly, Ng, J. Q. (2023) explained that external factors like free shipping

promotions and quality of service attracted consumers to impulse buying, especially during the

festival season.

H1: Psychological traits are a driving force significantly influencing impulsive buying decisions.

H2: Behavioral patterns are a driving force strongly related to influencing impulsive buying

Research Design

Methodology

The research employs a descriptive quantitative design to evaluate the relationship

between the variables affecting impulse buyers of Gen Z. The research focused on identifying

the connections between internal and external factors and impulse-buying decisions.   A self-

administered questionnaire was developed using a 5-point Likert scale to measure participants'

agreement with statements related to their buying habits.   The structured questionnaire is

composed of four parts.   The first part is the demographic profile, and the second to last part is

the questions addressed to the three objectives.   This method allowed the researcher to

understand the underlying forces shaping impulsive buying behavior.

Population and Locale of the Study

The population for this research comprises Generation Z individuals aged 12 to 27 years

old; however, only 18 are respondents (born between 1997 and 2007, not 2012) residing and or

digital marketing trends such as online shopping.   Baguio City is a prominent urban center and

tourist destination in the northern Philippines, which features a diverse online retail outlet and

near-dropping stations relevant to examining impulse buying behaviors.

Data Gathering Instrument

The researchers developed a structured survey questionnaire organized into several parts

to answer the research objectives and to facilitate comprehensive data collection on impulse

buying behavior among Generation Z. The questionnaire underwent a rigorous validation

process, including assessments by three expert professionals in the field.   One came from the

academe, a marketing specialist, and social media influencers to ensure the content relevance and

clarity of the questionnaire.   The instrument's validity was evaluated using Aiken's V. Following

the suggested modifications; the questionnaire was systematically updated and revised to ensure

alignment with the research objectives.

The researcher used Slovin's formula to compute the sample size of the respondents from

a 1,000 total population.   The researcher conducted a pilot test involving 15 respondents to

evaluate the effectiveness and reliability of the questionnaire.   Reliability was tested using

Cronbach's alpha to confirm internal consistency.   The reliability test was conducted using SPSS

Version 23, a statistical measure to assess the internal consistency and reliability of the

instrument.   The pre-test results showed a Cronbach's Alpha value of 0.96, indicating excellent

reliability, meaning the test items are highly integrated in measuring the intended concept.

Because of the test item's high level of dependability, measurement error is decreased because

consistent findings are obtained.   This thorough validation and pre-test process ensures the

accuracy and reliability of the questionnaire to be used in gathering the necessary data from the

respondents.

Data Gathering Procedure

Considering the limited running time of the distribution of questionnaires, the researcher

decided to create Google Forms (GForms) to facilitate a massive number of respondents.   The

GForms were then shared in the different group chats of students, and they were encouraged to

do the same to their respective groups or peers to expedite the completion of data gathering.

The respondents were informed of their voluntary participation and strict compliance with data

privacy and that the data would be treated with high confidentiality.   Among the Gen Z

respondents, only those aged 18 and above are chosen to respond to the survey since minors need

parental consent.   Hence, they are not included as respondents.   Throughout the research

journey, ethics standards are highly observed and practiced as set by the university's ethics

committee.   419 respondents actively participated in face-to-face and GForms surveys for two

weeks.   The data gathered were summarized and submitted for statistical treatment.   Each part

of the questionnaire was tabulated using the SPSS Version 23.

Scope and Limitation

This study delves into the impulse buying habits of Gen Z consumers using social media

and tangible consumer goods, thus emphasizing digital platforms' impact on shaping Gen Z's

purchasing habits as impulsive buyers.   Services and physical stores are avenues for future

researchers to explore more about the impulsive buying behavior of consumers and other

categories of products and variables.   In order to accomplish its goals, the study operates within

a time constraint of five months (the effect of typhoons and cancelation of classes and special

events distract the gathering of data) to expedite the use of Gform aside from face-to-face,

considering the timeline of the academic year.   In this study, researchers will focus solely on

four social media platforms: TikTok Shop, Facebook Marketplace, Shopee, and Lazada.   These

platforms were chosen for their prominence in online shopping and relevance to consumer

behavior.
