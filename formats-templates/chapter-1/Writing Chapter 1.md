**Chapter 1 \- Writing Guidelines** 

Each major section of Chapter 1 should be **concise and limited to approximately 4–5 paragraphs**. Each paragraph should discuss one main idea and should logically lead to the succeeding paragraph. 

**I. Introduction**

The Introduction should present the **general background of the study** using a general-to-specific approach. It should not immediately focus on the beneficiary or provide a detailed description of the proposed system.

**Paragraph 1 – Industry Background.** Introduce the industry or field and explain its importance, products, services, or general operations.

*Example:*  
The flower industry provides fresh flowers and floral arrangements for various personal, social, and commercial occasions. Flowers are commonly purchased for weddings, birthdays, anniversaries, graduations, funerals, and other celebrations. The continuous demand for floral products requires flower businesses to maintain sufficient varieties and quantities while ensuring that products remain fresh and suitable for customers.

**Paragraph 2 – Industry Challenges.** Discuss common problems or challenges associated with the industry and related to the proposed study.

*Example:*  
Unlike many retail products, fresh flowers are highly perishable and have a limited shelf life. Their quality may deteriorate because of storage duration, handling, temperature, and other environmental conditions. Flower businesses must therefore balance product availability with the risk of maintaining excessive stocks that may remain unsold and eventually become unsuitable for sale.

**Paragraph 3 – Role of Technology.** Discuss how information systems, automation, data analysis, or other technologies can help address the general challenges.

*Example:*  
Information systems can assist businesses in maintaining organized and updated records of inventory and sales transactions. Digital inventory systems can provide better visibility of available stocks, incoming and outgoing products, and historical transactions. The availability of organized historical data can also support the application of forecasting techniques for better inventory planning.

**Paragraph 4 – Specific Area of Concern.** Narrow the discussion toward the particular problem addressed by the proposed study.

*Example:*  
For flower businesses, inventory management becomes more challenging because customer demand may change according to seasons, holidays, and special occasions. Overstocking may increase the possibility of flower spoilage, while insufficient inventory may result in unavailable products and missed sales opportunities. Monitoring spoilage together with historical demand may therefore support more effective inventory decisions.

**Paragraph 5 – Need for the Study.** Establish the need for the study and transition to the Project Context.

*Example:*  
These conditions demonstrate the need for a more systematic approach to managing perishable flower inventory. Integrating inventory records, spoilage monitoring, and demand forecasting may provide flower businesses with relevant information for determining appropriate stock levels and minimizing unnecessary product losses.

**II. Project Context**

The Project Context should focus on the **specific beneficiary, existing process, identified problems, and proposed solution**. Problems presented in this section should preferably be based on interviews, observations, business records, or preliminary investigation.

**Paragraph 1 – Beneficiary and Existing Process.** Introduce the beneficiary and explain how the relevant activities are currently performed.

*Example:*  
**\[Name of Flower Shop\]** offers fresh flowers and customized floral arrangements for different occasions. The business regularly purchases different varieties of flowers and maintains available stocks to accommodate customer orders. Currently, information concerning incoming stocks, available quantities, sales, and discarded flowers is recorded through **\[state the actual existing process, such as notebooks or spreadsheets\]**.

**Paragraph 2 – Identified Problems.** Explain the **specific difficulties** encountered in the existing process.

*Example:*  
Based on the preliminary assessment of the existing process, the business experiences difficulty in monitoring how long particular flower stocks have remained in inventory. Records of available stocks and spoiled flowers are not maintained in a centralized system, making it difficult for personnel to immediately determine which stocks should be prioritized for sale or use. **\[list all the possible problems that may lead to your proposed system features\]**.

**Paragraph 3 – Effects of the Problems.** Explain the **consequences** of the identified problems.

*Example:*  
The lack of timely inventory information may contribute to unnecessary purchasing, stock shortages, and excessive quantities of certain flowers. When flowers remain unsold beyond their useful shelf life, the business may experience increased wastage and financial losses. Limited historical information may also make it difficult for management to estimate how much stock should be purchased for future periods.

**Paragraph 4 – Proposed System.** Introduce the proposed project and connect its features directly to the identified problems.

*Example:*  
To address these concerns, the study proposes the development of **FLOWERTRACK**, an inventory management system designed to record flower stocks, monitor stock movements, maintain sales records, and track potential flower spoilage. The system will provide centralized inventory information that can assist personnel in identifying available and aging flower stocks.

**Paragraph 5 – Algorithm or Technology.** Explain the role of the selected algorithm or model.

*Example:*  
FLOWERTRACK will also incorporate the **Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA)** model to forecast future flower demand using historical sales data. ARIMA will not directly determine whether flowers are spoiled; instead, its forecasts will support inventory planning by providing estimates of future demand. This may help management determine appropriate purchasing quantities and reduce excessive stocks that could eventually contribute to spoilage.

**III. Purpose and Description of the Study**

This section explains **why the system will be developed, what it will do, who will use it, and how the selected technology contributes to its operation**.

**Paragraph 1 – Purpose of the Study.** State the primary purpose of developing the system.

*Example:*  
The purpose of this study is to develop FLOWERTRACK, an inventory management system intended to improve the monitoring and management of flower stocks at **\[Name of Flower Shop\]**. The system aims **\[match it to the general objectives of the study\]** to provide organized inventory information, support flower spoilage monitoring, and provide demand forecasts that can assist management in inventory planning.

**Paragraph 2 – Description and Major Functions.** Describe the system and its major features.

*Example:*  
FLOWERTRACK will provide functions for recording incoming flower stocks, monitoring available quantities, recording stock-out transactions, maintaining sales records, and identifying stocks approaching their expected shelf-life limits. Authorized users will be able to access inventory information and generate relevant reports for monitoring business operations.

**Paragraph 3 – Algorithm and Processing.** Explain how the selected model or technology will function within the system.

*Example:*  
The system will utilize historical sales data as input to the ARIMA forecasting model. Based on identified time-series patterns, the model will generate forecasts of expected flower demand for succeeding periods. These forecasts will serve as supporting information for determining inventory requirements and purchasing decisions.

**Paragraph 4 – Expected Contribution.** Discuss the expected improvements resulting from the project.

*Example:*  
Through the integration of inventory management, spoilage monitoring, and demand forecasting, FLOWERTRACK is expected to improve inventory visibility and provide more reliable information for decision-making. The system may assist the business in reducing unnecessary overstocking, minimizing flower wastage, maintaining appropriate stock availability, and improving the overall management of perishable flower products.

**IV. Scope and Limitations**

The Scope and Limitations should clearly establish **what is included and excluded from the proposed project**.

**Paragraph 1** – **Research Locale, Beneficiary, and Users.** Identify where the study will be conducted, the organization or beneficiary involved, and the intended users of the proposed system. Briefly explain why the selected organization is relevant to the study.

Example:

The study will be conducted at \[Name of Flower Shop\], located in \[Barangay, City/Municipality, Province\], which will serve as the research locale and beneficiary of the proposed system. The study will focus on the flower shop's inventory-related operations, particularly the management of incoming and outgoing flower stocks, sales records, and spoiled products. The intended users of FLOWERTRACK will include the business owner, inventory personnel, and other authorized employees involved in inventory and sales management.

**Paragraph 2 – Functional Scope.** Identify the modules and components functions included.

*Example:*  
The system will cover the recording of flower stock-in and stock-out transactions, available inventory quantities, sales transactions, spoilage records, and inventory reports. It will also provide monitoring or notifications for flower stocks approaching their established shelf-life limits and allow users to review historical inventory and sales information.

**Paragraph 3 – Technical and Forecasting Scope.** Define the use and boundaries of the selected algorithm or technology.

*Example:*  
FLOWERTRACK will utilize the ARIMA model to forecast demand based on available historical sales data. Forecasts will be generated for flower products that contain sufficient historical observations for time-series analysis. Forecast results will be presented as decision-support information and will not automatically determine the quantity that management must purchase.

**Paragraph 4 – Limitations.** State the realistic boundaries and constraints of the system.

*Example:*  
The accuracy of the demand forecasts will depend on the completeness, quality, and amount of historical sales data available to the system. The forecasting model may not fully anticipate unexpected events or sudden changes in customer demand. Furthermore, spoilage monitoring will be based on predefined shelf-life information and recorded inventory data and will not involve laboratory testing, sensors, or automated biological detection of flower freshness unless these technologies are specifically included in the project.

**V. Operational Definition of Terms**

This section does not need to follow the 4–5 paragraph format. Terms should preferably be arranged **alphabetically** and defined according to **how they are specifically used in the study**.

**Example:**

**ARIMA (Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average).** In this study, it refers to the time-series forecasting model used by FLOWERTRACK to analyze historical flower sales data and generate forecasts of future demand.

**Demand Forecasting.** Refers to the process of estimating future demand for flower products using historical sales data processed through the ARIMA model.

**Flower Spoilage.** Refers to the deterioration of flower quality that may make the product unsuitable for sale or use based on the criteria established by the study.

**Note:**

The sections of Chapter 1 must be **connected and consistent**. Do not introduce features simply because they appear technologically impressive. Every major feature should address an identified problem.

For example:

**Problem:** Excessive flower stocks may result in spoilage.	  
**System Function:** Inventory and spoilage monitoring.	  
**Supporting Technology:** ARIMA forecasts expected demand.	  
**Expected Result:** Better inventory planning and reduced risk of overstocking.

**Most importantly, do not invent problems experienced by the beneficiary. Statements about the organization's actual difficulties should be supported by interviews, observations, records, surveys, or other appropriate data-gathering methods.**
