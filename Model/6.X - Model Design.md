### 1. Model Candidates & Selection Rationale

The literature presents a clear hierarchy of model families, each with distinct strengths and selection rationales.

- **Tree-Based Ensemble Models (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost):**
    - **Rationale:** These are the workhorses of predictive modeling for tabular data. They are favored for their high predictive accuracy, ability to handle non-linear relationships and feature interactions without extensive preprocessing, and **significant computational efficiency** compared to deep learning models. XGBoost and LightGBM are often cited as top performers, with LightGBM being particularly efficient.
    - **Quote:** *"Tree-based models are on average 126,934.94% faster to train than deep learning models"* (Hall & Rasheed, 2025).

- **Deep Learning Models (LSTM, GRU, Transformers):**
    - **Rationale:** These are the primary choice for **sequential data** like time-series spending patterns. LSTMs and GRUs are specifically designed to capture long-term dependencies, which is critical for understanding user spending behavior over weeks or months. Hybrid models (e.g., CNN-LSTM) and Transformer variants (e.g., Informer, Autoformer) are explored for even greater accuracy, especially for very long sequences.
    - **Quote:** *"LSTM networks are effective for capturing temporal dependencies in sequential financial data"* (Zlobin & Bazylevych, 2025).

- **Statistical Models (ARIMA, SARIMA, Exponential Smoothing):**
    - **Rationale:** These serve as essential **baselines and benchmarks**. They are preferred when **interpretability is paramount** and data patterns are relatively stable and seasonal. They are computationally inexpensive and require less data than deep learning models.
    - **Quote:** *"Statistical models like MLR and SARIMA are effective for short-term forecasting due to interpretability and low data demands"* (Saeedian, 2025).

- **Model Selection is a Trade-off:**
    The choice between these model families hinges on a core trade-off between **accuracy, computational cost, and interpretability**. Deep learning offers state-of-the-art accuracy but at a high computational cost and with "black-box" opacity, while a simpler model like linear regression is transparent and fast but may be less accurate.

### 2. Performance Targets / Success Metrics

The literature employs a multi-layered approach to evaluating forecasting model performance.

- **Prediction Error Metrics:**
    - **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** Measures the average magnitude of errors in the same unit as the target variable. Easy to interpret.
    - **Root Mean Square Error (RMSE):** Penalizes large errors more heavily than MAE, making it sensitive to outliers.
    - **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE):** Provides a scale-independent relative error metric, useful for comparing performance across different scales.

- **Classification Performance Metrics:**
    - **Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-Score:** Used when the forecasting task is framed as a classification problem (e.g., predicting if a user will churn or default).
    - **ROC-AUC & PR-AUC (Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve):** For imbalanced datasets (e.g., fraud detection), PR-AUC is often preferred over ROC-AUC as it provides a more informative measure of performance on the minority class.

- **Business & Operational Metrics:**
    - **User Satisfaction:** e.g., a user satisfaction rating of 4.4/5 for a personal finance management system.
    - **Operational Cost Reduction:** e.g., a **78% reduction** in manual expense-logging effort.
    - **Forecast Error Reduction:** e.g., machine learning models reducing prediction errors by **22-33%** compared to heuristic forecasts.
    - **Savings Improvement:** e.g., an AI-powered system leading to a **22% improvement in savings** among users.

### 3. Feature Engineering & Thresholds

Feature engineering is a critical step that often has a larger impact on performance than the choice of the model itself.

- **Key Feature Engineering Techniques:**
    - **Temporal Features:** Extracting components from timestamps, such as the hour of the day, day of the week, month of the year, and holiday flags. This is essential for capturing seasonal and cyclical patterns.
    - **Lag Features:** Using the value of a variable from previous time steps (e.g., spending from the last 7 days) as input features.
    - **Rolling Statistics:** Calculating moving averages, standard deviations, and other summary statistics over a recent window of data (e.g., the average spending over the past 30 days).
    - **External Data Integration:** Incorporating external signals like macroeconomic indicators, weather data, or sentiment from social media.
    - **Quote:** *"Quality of data and feature engineering overshadow incremental benefits of hyperparameter tuning"* (Hall, 2025).

- **Threshold Setting:**
    - In classification tasks, the decision threshold (e.g., the probability above which an instance is classified as "positive") is a key lever.
    - **Trade-off Management:** Lowering the threshold increases **Recall** (catching more positives) but increases false positives. Raising it increases **Precision** (fewer false alarms). The optimal threshold is business-context dependent.
    - **Imbalanced Data:** For highly imbalanced problems like fraud detection, methods like class weighting or cost-sensitive learning are used to adjust the model's sensitivity to the minority class.

---

### 4. Feature Selection & Rationale

Feature selection is the process of identifying and using only the most relevant features, reducing dimensionality and improving model performance.

- **Common Feature Selection Methods:**
    - **Filter Methods:** (e.g., **Correlation-based selection**) Select features based on their statistical relationship with the target variable. Fast but independent of the model.
    - **Wrapper Methods:** (e.g., **Recursive Feature Elimination (RFE)**) Use a model to score features and iteratively remove the weakest ones.
    - **Embedded Methods:** (e.g., **LASSO Regression**) Perform feature selection during model training by penalizing the weights of less important features to zero.
    - **Heuristic Algorithms:** (e.g., **Genetic Algorithms (GA)**) Use evolutionary search strategies to find the optimal subset of features.

- **Rationale & Evidence:**
    - **Performance Gains:** Feature selection can significantly boost model performance. For example, using a genetic algorithm to select features improved the **AUC from 50% to 71%** for a logistic regression model.
    - **Efficiency Gains:** Removing irrelevant features speeds up model training and inference.
    - **Interpretability:** A smaller, well-selected set of features makes the model easier to understand and explain.
    - **Identifying Key Drivers:** It helps isolate the most important predictors, such as identifying that **"Credit History," "Applicant Income," and "Loan Amount"** are the top predictors for loan approval.

---

### 5. Cold-Start Hypothesis

The "cold-start" problem refers to the challenge of making accurate predictions for new users or items for which there is no historical data.

- **Core Hypothesis:** New entities (users or items) can be modeled by leveraging **similarity** to existing entities or by using any **available auxiliary information** (e.g., demographics, user-provided preferences, item attributes).

- **Strategies to Mitigate the Cold-Start Problem:**
    - **Collaborative Filtering:** The hypothesis that users with similar past behaviors will have similar future behaviors. This works once the system has some initial data.
    - **Content-Based Filtering:** Uses the features of the new entity itself. For a new user, this could be their age, occupation, or stated financial goals.
    - **Hybrid Approaches:** Combining collaborative and content-based filtering to leverage both user and item features.
    - **Generative & Pre-training Approaches:** A newer strategy is to use **Large Language Models (LLMs)** or **Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)** to generate synthetic data that simulates the behavior of new users, allowing a model to be pre-trained before any real data is available.
    - **Quote:** *"Synthetic data enables robust model development with limited real data"* (Agrawal et al., 2025).

---

### 6. Concept Drift & Assumptions

Concept drift is the phenomenon where the statistical properties of the target variable and its relationship with the input features change over time.

- **Core Assumptions:**
    - **Inevitability:** In dynamic environments, concept drift is not an exception but a rule. User spending patterns, economic conditions, and market behavior are all subject to change.
    - **Performance Decay:** If left unaddressed, concept drift will inevitably lead to a decline in model accuracy and reliability. One study notes that **91% of ML models experience performance decay in production**, sometimes within days (Hasan et al., 2025).

- **Detection & Adaptation Strategies:**
    - **Drift Detection Algorithms:** Methods like **ADWIN** and **DDM** monitor the data stream in real-time to detect significant changes in the error rate or data distribution.
    - **Model Retraining:** The most common approach is to regularly retrain the model on a more recent window of data.
    - **Adaptive Learning Models:** Some algorithms, like online learning models or ensemble methods with adaptive weighting, can inherently adapt to change.
    - **Severity-Aware Adaptation:** More advanced frameworks assess the severity of the drift and choose an appropriate response—for example, performing a lightweight "fine-tune" for mild drift and a full retraining for severe drift.
    - **Quote:** *"Continuous curation and periodic retraining maintain model performance near peak levels (sawtooth pattern)"* (Hasan et al., 2025).

The literature reveals a broad consensus on several model classes, with selection depending heavily on data characteristics, task type, and deployment constraints.

**Tree-Based Models (XGBoost, LightGBM, Random Forest)**
- **Rationale:** Strongly recommended for tabular data, offering an excellent balance of performance, interpretability, and computational efficiency. They are particularly effective at capturing non-linear relationships and interactions.
- **Evidence:** Papers 14, 59, 85, and 88 extensively benchmark these models. **Paper 85** finds that **XGBoost** provides the best balance between forecasting accuracy and computational efficiency for demand forecasting. **Paper 14** shows it achieving the highest F1 score (0.680) and AUC (0.872) for predicting purchase behavior. **Paper 59** demonstrates its superior performance in student retention prediction. The survey in **Paper 88** concludes that tree-based models (especially LightGBM and CatBoost) are top performers and are **on average over 126,000% faster to train** than deep learning models, making them ideal for resource-constrained or mobile-first environments.

**Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM, GRU, BiLSTM)**
- **Rationale:** The standard choice for sequential data (e.g., time series of spending) due to their ability to learn long-term dependencies and temporal patterns. LSTM is the most cited and widely used variant.
- **Evidence:** Papers 3, 7, 27, 64, 83, and 93 consistently show LSTM achieving high accuracy in forecasting tasks. **Paper 3** reports an LSTM model achieving **99.93% accuracy** in expense prediction. **Paper 27** finds LSTM achieves a **4.7% Mean Absolute Error** for monthly expenditure prediction. **Paper 93** shows an LSTM with a self-attention mechanism achieving **94.2% accuracy** in predicting consumer purchase intentions. The survey in **Paper 69** confirms LSTM's effectiveness for time series forecasting and anomaly detection.

**Hybrid and Ensemble Models**
- **Rationale:** Combining different model classes (e.g., LSTM + Logistic Regression, ARIMA + LSTM) consistently yields superior performance by leveraging the strengths of each. This is a dominant theme in the literature for robust and accurate forecasting.
- **Evidence:**
    - **Paper 65** proposes a hybrid LSTM + Logistic Regression model for credit default prediction. The hybrid model achieved an **AUC-ROC of 0.91**, outperforming LSTM-only (0.85) and Logistic Regression (0.81), demonstrating improved accuracy and interpretability.
    - **Paper 53** highlights that **hybrid ARIMA-LSTM frameworks** improve forecasting robustness by combining linear and non-linear modeling.
    - **Paper 83** benchmarks hybrid architectures (e.g., LSTM-GRU) and finds they often show superior descriptive performance and stability compared to single architectures, though the Friedman test found no statistically significant differences across nine architectures.
    - **Paper 88** notes that model combination and ensemble methods consistently improve predictive performance.

**Selection Rationale Summary:**
- **For structured, tabular spending data:** Prioritize XGBoost, LightGBM, or Random Forest due to high accuracy, interpretability, and exceptional computational efficiency.
- **For sequential, time-series spending data:** Use LSTM, GRU, or BiLSTM, especially when modeling long-term dependencies or complex temporal patterns.
- **For best possible performance:** Adopt a hybrid approach (e.g., ARIMA-LSTM, LSTM-Logistic Regression, or an ensemble of tree-based and deep learning models).
- **For handling non-stationarity and concept drift:** Adaptive learning frameworks, including incremental learning (e.g., EFRT-DD, CDR in **Paper 44**) and offline RL (e.g., CQL in **Paper 49**), are emerging as effective strategies.

## 2. Performance Targets / Success Metrics

The literature strongly recommends a multi-metric evaluation framework, as no single metric captures all aspects of forecasting performance. The key metrics and targets are:

- **Primary Forecasting Accuracy Metrics:**
    - **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** A robust, intuitive metric that is less sensitive to outliers than RMSE. Desirable values depend on the scale of spending, but achieving a low MAE is a primary goal.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 27** achieves a **4.7% MAE** for monthly expenditure prediction. **Paper 64** reports LSTM achieving a **5.4% MAPE**.
    - **Root Mean Square Error (RMSE):** Penalizes large errors more heavily, making it valuable when large deviations are particularly costly.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 83** shows LSTM-GRU achieving an RMSE of 23.205 for sunspot forecasting.
    - **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE):** Provides a scale-independent measure, useful for comparing accuracy across users with different spending levels.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 99** reports a PSO-optimized NGBM(1,1) model achieving a **5.45% out-of-sample MAPE** for Philippine GDP nowcasting. **Paper 83** shows GRU-LSTM achieving a **36.242% MAPE** for sunspot data, while standalone LSTM achieved **9.036% MAPE** for COVID-19 case prediction, highlighting the domain-specific nature of achievable targets.

- **Classification and Ranking Metrics (for tasks like risk profiling or anomaly detection):**
    - **AUC-ROC / AUC-PR:** Widely used for evaluating model discrimination in classification tasks like credit risk or fraud detection, especially with imbalanced data.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 65** reports a hybrid model achieving an **AUC-ROC of 0.91** for default prediction. **Paper 45** shows a hybrid Wide & Deep + CNN model achieving a **perfect ROC-AUC of 1.0000**.
    - **Precision, Recall, F1-Score:** Essential for tasks where the cost of false positives and false negatives differs.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 96** reports an anomaly detection system achieving **92% accuracy**, with **90% precision** and **85% recall**, yielding an **F1-score of 87.5%**.

- **System and Economic Metrics:**
    - **Latency and Efficiency:** Critical for real-time and mobile-first applications.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 71** shows that hybrid routing can reduce inference latency by up to **55%** compared to deep learning alone. **Paper 78** achieves end-to-end inference averaging **18 milliseconds** on CPU-only hardware.
    - **Economic Significance:** Models showing statistical significance may not translate to economic value.
        - **Targets:** **Paper 60** notes that 60-80% of published anomalies fail to replicate, and models often fail to generate economic value after accounting for transaction costs, resulting in net performance reductions of **15-40%**.

## 3. Feature Engineering & Thresholds

## 5. Cold-Start Hypothesis

The "cold-start" problem (predicting behavior for new users with limited or no historical data) is a major theme, with several strategies proposed:

- **Data Augmentation:** Generating synthetic data to bootstrap models for new users.
    - **Evidence:** **Paper 77** proposes PAnDA, a framework using LLMs to generate preference-aligned augmented data for cold-start recommendations, achieving a **37.55% improvement in Recall@10** on a sparse dataset. **Paper 47** shows that small LLMs can be effective cold-start recommenders using only short interaction sequences.
- **Behavioral Profiling and Proxy Features:** Using initial user-declared preferences or demographic data as a proxy for behavior until sufficient transaction history is accumulated.
    - **Evidence:** **Paper 67** uses Big Five personality profiles in a hybrid recommender to mitigate cold-start, demonstrating improved user satisfaction and recommendation quality. **Paper 92** uses synthetic data generation to address data limitations for initial profile creation.
- **Alternative Data:** Using non-traditional data like mobile money transactions or employment history to assess new users.
    - **Evidence:** **Paper 37** highlights that using alternative data enabled 58% of borrowers with no formal credit history to be identified as low or moderate risk, directly addressing the cold-start problem for credit scoring. **Paper 12** shows that behavioral financial data (e.g., mobile money) can predict creditworthiness.
- **Default Baselines:** Using simple, interpretable models (like logistic regression or exponential smoothing) as a fallback for new users, before more complex models can be trained.
    - **Evidence:** **Paper 70** notes that simple models like official forecasts, random walk, and regression remain relevant benchmarks and often perform comparably to complex models when data is limited.

**Key Finding:** The literature emphasizes that the cold-start problem is best addressed by a combination of strategies. A hybrid approach—using proxy features or synthetic data for initial estimates, and then dynamically updating user profiles as actual data becomes available—is likely the most effective.

## 6. Concept Drift & Assumptions

The literature treats concept drift (changes in data distributions over time) as a **fundamental challenge**, not an edge case. There is a strong consensus that static models are fragile and require continuous monitoring and adaptation.

- **Assumption:** The core assumption of most forecasting models (distributional stationarity) is **fundamentally invalid** for financial data. This is considered the norm, not an exception.
    - **Evidence:** **Paper 9** explicitly states that "non-stationarity is the norm in financial data, not the exception." **Paper 97** reports that **91% of ML models** degrade in production, often within days.

- **Drift Handling Strategies:**
    - **Continuous Monitoring and Retraining:** Dynamic model updating is essential to maintain performance.
        - **Evidence:** **Paper 29** shows that dynamic retraining with a sliding window improves AUC by up to 5 percentage points during volatile periods. **Paper 30** finds that **ADWIN with incremental learning** dominates on gradual drift, while **DDM with retraining** reacts fastest to abrupt shifts, recommending a hybrid approach.
    - **Adaptive Learning:** Using models that inherently adapt to new data, such as incremental learners (EFRT-DD, **Paper 44**) and reinforcement learning frameworks (ARL-CPO, **Paper 42**).
        - **Evidence:** **Paper 44** shows that the collaborative framework (CDR) combining incremental and batch models improves RMSE by **3.61%** and MAE by **14.77%** over a state-of-the-art incremental tree. **Paper 42** reports a reinforcement learning framework achieving a **98.8% trend adaptation rate**.
    - **Explicit Drift Detection:** Implementing algorithms (ADWIN, DDM, Page-Hinkley) to trigger model retraining or structural updates.
        - **Evidence:** **Paper 34** benchmarks drift detectors and finds **SEED and STEPD** consistently outperform others. **Paper 76** identifies **SVM** as the most effective learner for detecting and adapting to concept drift in time-series.
    - **Robust Evaluation:** Validating models under drift is critical. Standard cross-validation can be misleading.
        - **Evidence:** **Paper 9** states that "out-of-time validation and calibration metrics are essential, yet often overlooked, for reliable evaluation." **Paper 60** recommends purged cross-validation to prevent information leakage.

- **Key Recommendation:** A hierarchical architecture that combines:
    1.  **Lightweight screening at the edge** (e.g., using an efficient drift detector) to flag potential shifts.
    2.  **More rigorous validation and model updating in the cloud** or a more powerful backend system.
    3.  **A feedback loop** to refine the model and re-deploy it, ensuring continuous reliability and performance. (**Paper 26** provides a concrete example of this architecture, reporting a 40.6% reduction in prediction MAE compared to periodic retraining.)

|---|---|---|
