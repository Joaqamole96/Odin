## 6. Concept Drift & Assumptions

### Definition & Importance

"Non-stationarity is the norm in financial data, not the exception"【19†L120】. Concept drift refers to "change in joint distribution P(X,y) over time"【226†L55】, which degrades predictive model performance【73†L45】.

### Drift Types

| Type | Definition | Relevance | Source |
|------|------------|-----------|--------|
| **Covariate drift** | Change in P(X), P(y\|X) constant | Spending patterns shift | Pai et al. (2024)【226†L55】 |
| **Actual drift** | Change in P(y\|X), P(X) constant | Behavior-outcome relationship changes | Pai et al. (2024)【226†L55】 |
| **Concept drift** | Change in joint distribution | Most challenging | Caroprese et al. (2025)【141†L55】 |
| **Recurrent drift** | Seasonal spending changes | Explicit example: seasonal spending | Khan & Sadaoui (2026)【73†L50】 |

### Detection Methods

| Method | Performance | Source |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **EWMA + HT** | AUC ≈ 0.69, best SPC performance | Khan & Sadaoui (2026)【73†L55】 |
| **ARF + HT** | AUC ≈ 0.94 on abrupt synthetic drifts | Khan & Sadaoui (2026)【73†L55】 |
| **AUE + HT** | AUC ≈ 0.88 on real-world streams | Khan & Sadaoui (2026)【73†L55】 |
| **HDDM_W** | Triggers retraining when drift detected | Caroprese et al. (2025)【141†L55】 |
| **Embedding cosine distance** | Detects gradual and recurring changes | Martin et al. (2023)【271†L55】 |

### Adaptation Strategies

| Strategy | Effectiveness | Source |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| **Adaptive Fusion** | 95% accuracy, ROC-AUC >0.96 under drift | Chikoore et al. (2026)【24†L55】 |
| **Lifelong learning (GEM)** | OldTransfer 0.1785 vs finetune 0.0 | Pai et al. (2024)【226†L55】 |
| **Drift-aware continual learning** | Mean macro-F1 improved 0.706→0.742 | Rajulapati et al. (2026)【12†L55】 |
| **Adaptive SHAP** | Reduces demographic parity difference by 0.026 | John (2026)【5†L50】 |

### Key Assumptions & Findings

1. **Drift is inevitable**: "Seasonal changes in spending behavior are a type of recurrent drift that Odin must detect"【73†L50】

2. **Lifelong learning preserves past knowledge**: "Lifelong learning methods, especially GEM, outperform others in preserving old knowledge across all drift types"【226†L55】

3. **Evaluation must account for drift**: "Out-of-time validation and calibration metrics are essential for reliable model evaluation"【30†L80】

4. **Hybrid adaptation works best**: "Hybrid adaptation architectures, combining incremental updates with event-driven interventions, balance responsiveness and stability"【19†L55】

5. **Detector selection depends on drift type**: "Ensemble-based detectors like ARF with Hoeffding Trees achieve the highest accuracy on abrupt drifts"【73†L55】

### Performance Under Drift

- **Without drift adaptation**: Recall dropped to 89.8% under mimicry attacks【91†L55】
- **With drift adaptation**: Recovered to 95.2% within two days【91†L55】
- **Drift-aware continual learning**: Reduced temporal variance (std from 0.030 to 0.015)【12†L55】
- **Subgroup fairness gaps**: Decreased from 0.118 to 0.082 under drift-aware updating【12†L55】

# 5.B

Based on the compiled research summaries, here is a synthesis of the evidence regarding key technical aspects of financial behavioral profiling for a system like Odin.

### Model Candidates & Selection Rationale

The literature presents a diverse set of candidate models for behavioral profiling, with the choice often depending on the specific task (e.g., classification, forecasting, segmentation) and data characteristics.

*   **Ensemble Methods (XGBoost, Random Forest):** These are frequently recommended for classification tasks due to their high predictive accuracy and robustness.
    *   **XGBoost** is highlighted as a top performer in various financial classification tasks, including credit risk【35†L49】【49†L49】, churn prediction【89†L16】, and marketing response【98†L16】. It often outperforms traditional models like logistic regression【83†L16】. In one study, XGBoost with BorderlineSMOTE2 achieved an F1-score of 0.87【98†L72】.
    *   **Random Forest** is another strong ensemble candidate, noted for its ability to handle imbalanced data and provide feature importance【52†L16】【68†L16】. It has been used effectively for customer segmentation【124†L16】 and predicting behavioral intention【135†L16】.

*   **Deep Learning Models (LSTM, CNN, Transformers):** These are favored for tasks involving sequential data, such as spending forecasting and anomaly detection.
    *   **LSTM** networks are a standard choice for time-series forecasting【92†L33】【130†L16】【132†L16】, with one study showing them achieving a MAPE of 4.82% compared to 20.97% for ARIMA【132†L136】.
    *   **Transformers** and **Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)** are emerging as powerful tools for capturing complex patterns in sequential and relational data, such as transaction sequences and customer identity networks【110†L33】【110†L41】.

*   **Clustering Algorithms (K-Means, HDBSCAN, Spectral Clustering):** For unsupervised segmentation, K-Means is the most dominant algorithm【124†L72】. However, more advanced methods like **HDBSCAN** (which handles varying densities)【105†L19】 and **Spectral Clustering** (which finds non-globular clusters)【120†L28】 are recommended when data shapes are irregular.

*   **Hybrid and Adaptive Models:** A recurring theme is that hybrid approaches, which combine multiple models or techniques, often outperform single models. Examples include combining clustering with classification【119†L16】, or using a "glass-box" model like Explainable Boosting Machine (EBM) after feature selection by a black-box model like XGBoost to maintain interpretability【108†L30】.

### Performance Targets / Success Metrics

The choice of evaluation metrics is critical, especially given the imbalanced nature of many financial datasets (e.g., fraud detection, churn prediction).

*   **For Classification Tasks:** **F1-score** and **AUC-ROC** are strongly recommended over accuracy, which can be misleading when classes are imbalanced【52†L29】【89†L16】. The F1-score, as the harmonic mean of precision and recall, is highlighted as the most stable and balanced metric【52†L29】. AUC-ROC values above 0.80 are considered indicative of robust model performance【89†L16】.
*   **For Clustering and Segmentation:** Internal validation metrics like the **Silhouette Score** and **Davies-Bouldin Index** are commonly used to assess cluster quality【120†L31】【124†L22】. A Silhouette Score above 0.5 is generally considered good, with some studies achieving scores as high as 0.91【120†L78】.
*   **For Forecasting:** **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)** is a standard metric for evaluating forecast accuracy【130†L65】.
*   **For User-Centric Evaluation:** Metrics like **Precision@k** and **Recall@k** are used to assess the quality of top-K recommendations【114†L68】. Additionally, user studies measuring **subjective understanding**, **response confidence**, and **trust** are crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of explanations and system design【99†L36】.

### Feature Engineering & Thresholds

Feature engineering is a cornerstone of effective profiling, with many studies deriving powerful predictors from raw transaction data.

*   **Key Behavioral Features:** Commonly engineered features include **Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) metrics**【6†L68】【105†L49】, **income volatility**【10†L33】【118†L18】, **expense-to-income ratio**【139†L78】, and **loss-chasing behavior**【55†L23】. Including features like **customer cost** has been shown to improve segmentation【120†L27】.
*   **Income-Relative Thresholds:** Studies on financial harm recommend using income-relative thresholds, such as "depositing more than 2% of monthly income," as they are more predictive than absolute spend thresholds【8†L132】. Surpassing multiple such limits was found to exponentially increase the odds of financial harm【8†L130】.
*   **Domain-Specific Features:** For credit risk, features like **log growth rate of average balance** (with an Information Value of 0.484) have been found to be more predictive than traditional application data【19†L132】.

### Feature Selection & Rationale

Given the high dimensionality of financial data, feature selection is crucial for building interpretable and efficient models.

*   **SHAP for Feature Importance:** SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is the most frequently used method for identifying and ranking important features【69†L15】. It has been used to show that institutional trust is a stronger predictor of financial literacy than education【5†L16】, and that debt ratio is the most influential feature for predicting financial well-being【30†L43】.
*   **Dimensionality Reduction:** Techniques like **Genetic Algorithms (GA)** have been shown to significantly improve model performance by selecting optimal feature subsets. One study found that GA feature selection improved AUC by 21% over using all features【104†L108】. **Principal Component Analysis (PCA)** and **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)** are also used for dimensionality reduction【105†L42】【111†L16】.
*   **Rationale for Selection:** Feature selection is driven by the need for **interpretability** (especially in regulated domains like credit scoring)【108†L13】, **reduction of computational cost**【109†L20】, and **mitigation of overfitting**【52†L16】.

### Cold-Start Hypothesis

The cold-start problem—making accurate predictions for new users with no historical data—is a major challenge addressed by several studies.

*   **Synthetic Data Generation:** A primary strategy is to use **LLM-generated synthetic data** to pre-train models, providing a baseline for new users. This approach significantly reduced initial error and improved early-stage recommendation accuracy【54†L16】【51†L16】.
*   **Transfer Learning:** Frameworks like **TechCD** use a knowledge concept graph to transfer "student states" from a source domain to a zero-shot target domain, enabling profiling without target-domain interaction data【133†L18】. This is directly analogous to profiling a new user's financial behavior.
*   **Psychometric and Demographic Data:** When transaction history is unavailable, initial profiles can be built using **personality traits**【101†L16】【129†L16】, **demographic data**【127†L16】, or **psychometric indicators**【118†L16】. For instance, using Big Five personality traits was shown to mitigate the cold-start problem in financial recommendations【101†L55】.
*   **Hybrid Approaches:** Combining collaborative filtering (which fails with new users) with content-based or demographic data is a common strategy to address cold-start【71†L16】.

### Concept Drift & Assumptions

Financial behavior is non-stationary, meaning models must adapt to changes over time (concept drift).

*   **Dynamic Profiling is Essential:** Static models fail under non-stationary data distributions【134†L16】. Studies emphasize the need for models that can track behavioral evolution and suppress "unstable identity switching"【29†L16】.
*   **Drift Detection and Adaptation:** Techniques like **ADWIN** (a statistical drift detector) and **embedding-based drift detection** are used to identify when a model's performance is degrading due to distributional shifts【134†L39】. Upon detection, models can be updated via **retraining**【65†L16】 or **test-time adaptation**【26†L16】.
*   **Lifelong Learning vs. Domain Adaptation:** Research suggests that **lifelong learning** methods (which preserve old knowledge) are better for handling gradual drift, while **domain adaptation** excels at adapting to new covariate shifts but can degrade performance on past patterns【111†L22】.
*   **Assumptions about Drift:** Many models assume that concept drift is **gradual or incremental**【111†L16】, but **abrupt shifts** (e.g., due to a pandemic) are also common【100†L16】. The literature recommends using **ensemble methods** and **regularization techniques** like Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) to balance plasticity (adapting to new patterns) and stability (retaining past knowledge)【134†L21】.

### 1. Model Candidates & Selection Rationale

### 2. Performance Targets / Success Metrics

The research emphasizes that the choice of performance metrics must align with the business objective, particularly in imbalanced datasets where accuracy can be misleading.

*   **Classification Metrics**:
    *   **AUC-ROC and F1-Score** are the most frequently reported metrics for imbalanced classification tasks like fraud detection and churn prediction【2†L53】【141†L25】. A model achieving an AUC above 0.80 and F1 above 0.70 is considered robust【141†L50】.
    *   **Precision and Recall** are critical for anomaly detection, where the cost of false positives and false negatives must be balanced. For instance, a cost-sensitive neural network achieved a recall of 0.82 for fraud detection with a 10:1 cost ratio【123†L27】.
    *   **Weighted Accuracy (WA)** is proposed as a metric that aligns with total misclassification cost, ensuring model selection minimizes financial impact【207†L28】.

### 3. Feature Engineering & Thresholds

Feature engineering is a critical step, with studies highlighting the importance of deriving meaningful predictors from raw data.

*   **Common Feature Types**:
    *   **RFM (Recency, Frequency, Monetary)** features are widely used for customer segmentation and behavioral profiling【38†L24】【102†L27】.
    *   **Temporal features** such as transaction time, day of week, and seasonal indicators are crucial for capturing spending patterns【46†L64】【158†L28】.
    *   **Behavioral indicators** like spending volatility, impulse buying tendencies, and loss-chasing behavior are used for risk profiling【53†L24】【92†L27】.
    *   **Alternative data** such as mobile phone usage, utility payments, and psychometric scores are increasingly used for credit scoring【42†L26】【81†L34】.

*   **Thresholds**:
    *   **Risk thresholds** are defined based on probability of default (e.g., low <10%, moderate 10-25%, high >25%)【42†L35】.
    *   **Anomaly thresholds** are set using statistical methods like the 99th percentile of reconstruction error in autoencoders【132†L36】.
    *   **Cost-sensitive thresholds** are tuned to optimize the trade-off between false positives and false negatives. For example, a cost ratio of 10:1 for fraud detection improved recall from 0.58 to 0.82【123†L27】.

### 5. Cold-Start Hypothesis

The cold-start problem is a major focus, with studies proposing various strategies to handle new users with limited data.

*   **Synthetic Data Generation**: Using LLMs to generate synthetic user interactions is a promising approach. A study found that pre-training with GPT-generated data reduced initial prediction error by 25%【91†L36】.
*   **Transfer Learning**: A knowledge concept graph embedding framework (TechCD) enables zero-shot cognitive diagnosis by transferring user states from a source domain to a target domain【199†L25】.
*   **Personality-Based Profiling**: Using Big Five personality traits can mitigate the cold-start problem in recommendations. A hybrid model using personality profiles outperformed pure collaborative filtering【65†L25】.
*   **Demographic-Based Initialization**: Clustering users based on demographic data can provide a baseline for initial recommendations【204†L27】.

### 6. Concept Drift & Assumptions

Concept drift, the change in data distribution over time, is a critical challenge for maintaining model performance.

*   **Detection Methods**:
    *   **Adaptive Windowing (ADWIN)** and **CUSUM** are used for online drift detection【15†L42】【125†L28】.
    *   **Ensemble methods** like BIC and Adaptive Random Forest are robust to different drift types【125†L32】.

*   **Adaptation Strategies**:
    *   **Batch retraining** and **sliding window learning** are common, but **adaptive fusion** of multiple models (original, retrained, windowed) showed the most robust performance【20†L38】.
    *   **Lifelong learning methods** like GEM are effective at preserving old knowledge while adapting to new drift【173†L32】.

*   **Assumptions**:
    *   Studies assume that **behavioral patterns are not static** and that models must be continuously updated【15†L17】.
    *   **Seasonality** is a common assumption, with models incorporating features to capture cyclical spending patterns【197†L29】.
    *   The **performative prediction** framework assumes that model deployment itself can alter the data distribution, necessitating feedback-aware modeling【148†L26】.
